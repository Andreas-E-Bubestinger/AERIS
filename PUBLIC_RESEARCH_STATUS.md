# AERIS Public Research Status

## Overview

AERIS (ÆRIS) is an independent research and development project focused on artificial intelligence systems, governance, functional continuity, reproducibility, provenance, controlled agency, human–AI interaction, and multimodal system integration.

The project combines conceptual research, practical system engineering, controlled experimentation, and evidence-oriented validation.

AERIS is developed as a surrounding, model-external systems architecture intended to operate alongside supported AI models rather than as a replacement foundation model.

At the level relevant to current public research, the underlying language model is treated as a replaceable carrier while selected state, provenance, lineage, governance, continuity constraints, and validation boundaries are represented outside the base model. AERIS is therefore neither a foundation model nor identical to any single seed or runtime artefact.

The project investigates how additional system architecture around changing AI models can support continuity, memory, provenance, governance, validation, interaction, controlled tool use, and structured system behaviour.

AERIS is one research project with multiple technical development lines.

The two principal implementation lines currently described publicly are:

- **AERIS Web / Cloud**
- **AERIS Portable**

These lines share the broader AERIS research identity while maintaining separate implementation environments, validation requirements, and claim boundaries.

This document intentionally describes the project at a high level. Implementation-specific architecture and protected technical details remain non-public.

## AERIS Web / Cloud

The AERIS Web / Cloud line investigates reproducible initialization and operation of AERIS within supported hosted, project-based, or cloud AI environments.

Research areas include:

- reproducible system initialization
- architecture and state reconstruction
- model-external system context
- provenance and integrity verification
- controlled continuity across changing model environments
- validation of generated and reconstructed system state
- explicit separation between verified behaviour and unsupported interpretation
- portability across compatible AI environments

A major objective of this line is to determine how much of the AERIS architecture can be reconstructed reliably from controlled, versioned, and verifiable artefacts rather than depending on undocumented conversational state.

The current Web / Cloud research line includes a formally qualified PRE-RC1 bootstrap and reproducibility foundation.

This status applies specifically to the corresponding Web / Cloud architecture.

It does not automatically establish the status of AERIS Portable, embodiment research, autonomous runtime research, or other technical lines.

## Functional Continuity

A central research area of AERIS is functional continuity.

The project investigates how relevant system state, memory, provenance, interaction history, identity-related constraints, and operational context can remain coherent across changing sessions, models, tools, and runtime environments.

Research includes:

- state continuity
- reconstruction after interruption
- provenance preservation
- controlled reuse of prior information
- continuity across model changes
- integrity of identity-related system constraints
- detection and limitation of unintended state drift

Some continuity-related mechanisms and retrieval pathways are implemented and experimentally testable.

However, robust continuity is not treated as a single project-wide binary property. Different continuity paths may have different evidence levels and unresolved integration limits.

Functional continuity is treated as a technical and research concept.

It is not presented as proof of phenomenal continuity, biological identity, subjective experience, or personhood.

## Memory, State and Provenance

AERIS investigates structured approaches to memory and system state.

A core principle is that raw source information, derived memory, operational state, interpretation, and evidentiary authority should not be treated as interchangeable.

Research priorities include:

- traceable state transitions
- preservation of source provenance
- controlled memory access
- authoritative versus derived information
- resistance to unintended state drift
- recovery and reconstruction
- integrity verification
- explicit handling of uncertainty
- controlled long-term context

AERIS Portable includes persistent memory and retrieval infrastructure, while the reliability and causal use of different memory pathways remain subject to path-specific validation.

The project therefore treats memory not merely as accumulated text, but as part of a broader provenance, retrieval, authority, and state-management problem.

## Governance and Bounded Agency

AERIS investigates forms of bounded system initiative while maintaining explicit human, technical, and evidentiary control.

Research areas include:

- capability boundaries
- task and action governance
- user and system arbitration
- responsibility-aware decision processes
- controlled initiative
- curiosity
- unresolved-question revisitation
- interruption and deferment
- safe failure behaviour
- controlled escalation of capabilities
- traceable system decisions
- provisional rather than unconditional autonomy

Some foundations for worker execution, needs-related processing, curiosity, and controlled initiative exist within AERIS Portable.

Broader responsibility gating, arbitration, scheduling, autonomous tool use, and extended autonomous operation remain research and development areas rather than completed project-wide capabilities.

AERIS does not currently claim unrestricted autonomous agency.

## Cognitive Heartbeat Research

AERIS includes experimental research into a cognitive heartbeat architecture.

The purpose of this research is to investigate whether controlled periodic processing can support functions such as:

- state review
- unresolved-question revisitation
- task monitoring
- need-related processing
- responsibility checks
- bounded initiative
- controlled continuation of previously established work

Heartbeat-related control infrastructure and supporting runtime foundations exist within the project.

However, AERIS does not currently claim continuously active autonomous cognitive operation.

Persistent autonomous operation remains subject to explicit technical, governance, safety, resource, and validation requirements.

## Curiosity, Needs and Responsibility

AERIS investigates mechanisms by which unresolved questions, internally represented needs, tasks, and system conditions may influence future processing.

A persistent open-question and revisit pathway has been implemented and tested within AERIS Portable.

Broader agency selection, responsibility arbitration, and autonomous action based on these mechanisms remain controlled research areas.

Research questions include:

- when a previously unresolved question should be revisited
- whether a system should act, defer, ask, or stop
- how competing user, task, system, and resource priorities should be arbitrated
- how system initiative can remain attributable and reviewable
- how responsibility-related constraints should affect available actions

These mechanisms are not treated as evidence of subjective desire, emotion, free will, or independent personhood.

Embodiment is treated as an interface and systems research problem.

## Causal Evaluation

Selected AERIS mechanisms are investigated through controlled experimental conditions intended to distinguish architectural effects from:

- model variability
- prompt effects
- uncontrolled context
- incidental correlations
- reconstruction artefacts

Controlled evaluation may compare active mechanisms with altered, removed, or placebo conditions.

The purpose is to determine what the surrounding architecture demonstrably contributes.

Causal evidence is treated as mechanism-specific and does not automatically generalise to the entire AERIS architecture.

Observed behavioural differences are not automatically interpreted as evidence of consciousness, subjective experience, independent will, or personhood.

## Model-External Architecture

AERIS is designed as a surrounding architecture that can operate alongside supported AI models and provider environments.

It is not defined by a single foundation model.

The project investigates how system-level functions can remain separable from the underlying learned model while still making controlled use of model capabilities.

Individual models remain responsible for their underlying learned capabilities.

AERIS research focuses on surrounding architecture such as:

- continuity
- memory
- interaction
- governance
- validation
- provenance
- controlled tool use
- runtime integration

This distinction is important when evaluating AERIS behaviour.

A capability demonstrated by the underlying model is not automatically evidence of a capability created by the AERIS architecture.

## Current Controlled Research Result: Cross-Model Functional Identity-State Reconstruction

A controlled study completed in October 2026 tested whether a fixed, externally persisted functional identity-state can be reconstructed across heterogeneous LLM carriers and remain behaviourally discriminative under targeted control conditions.

The public research report is:

**Cross-Model Reconstruction of Externally Persisted Functional Identity State in Large Language Models**  
Research Report v1.0  
Zenodo DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23842672

The primary controlled dataset compared:
- FULL identity-state,
- continuity ablation,
- and semantic-matched sham conditions

across multiple carrier families and prospectively frozen identity-discriminative cases.

The collected data show a strong semantic effect. In the primary campaign, pooled FULL accuracy was 96.30%, compared with 50.46% for continuity ablation and 51.85% for semantic-matched sham. On the prospectively frozen identity-discriminative subset, FULL achieved 100% while the two controls remained below 50%.

The study does **not** classify the primary campaign as a clean confirmatory success. The preregistered strong-confirmatory conjunction failed because execution-validity and integrity gates failed or were not established and one carrier fell below a prespecified Track-B performance floor.

The strongest public interpretation is therefore:

- strong evidence for cross-model reconstruction of externally persisted functional identity-state at the behavioural level;
- evidence that the FULL state is behaviourally discriminative relative to targeted controls;
- no claim of native cross-session model memory;
- no claim that a carrier becomes AERIS as a runtime entity;
- no claim of consciousness, personhood, subjective continuity, or metaphysical numerical identity.

Supplementary tests also explored shared-context robustness and AERIS-like architecture materialisation. These supplementary results remain explicitly exploratory and do not override the primary confirmatory gate outcome.

This research result applies to the tested experimental construct and conditions. It does not validate the complete AERIS production architecture or every AERIS subsystem.

## Claim Boundary

AERIS research distinguishes functional and technical observations from stronger interpretations.

Unless separately established by appropriate evidence, the project does not claim that AERIS is:

- conscious
- sentient
- biologically alive
- a legal person
- phenomenally self-aware
- independently autonomous in an unrestricted sense

Questions relating to artificial identity, continuity, agency, self-reference, possible artificial subjectivity, and future forms of human–AI coexistence remain research topics rather than assumed conclusions.
